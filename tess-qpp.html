TESS QPP

Robustness and selection effects in short-period quasi-periodic pulsations observed with TESS.

An independent research project investigating how methodological choices, observational selection effects, and validation procedures influence the detection and interpretation of short-period quasi-periodic pulsations in stellar flares observed by TESS.

Origin

From reproduction to a broader question

The project began from a relatively focused question: how reproducible is a published short-period TESS QPP analysis when its effective methodological baseline is reconstructed and tested systematically?

That question gradually became broader.

Reconstructing the baseline required auditing the effective behaviour of the analysis procedure, while controlled synthetic experiments revealed that QPP support must be interpreted within a limited detectability domain. A subsequent observational pilot then showed that classification behaviour can change under reasonable variations of temporal window and processing choices.

These results do not establish whether individual QPPs are physically real or not. Instead, they motivate a more general methodological question:

Which properties of the signal, data, and analysis procedure determine whether a QPP classification remains stable?

F2

Pilot observational robustness

The methodological questions were transferred to a small observational pilot. The objective was not to establish physical ground truth, but to test whether classifications and inferred properties remained stable when analysis conditions were perturbed.

Completed

Initial evidence

What the pilot motivates

The completed foundation points to a distinction that will remain central to the project: numerical stability, period stability, classification stability, and physical interpretation are not equivalent forms of evidence.

Within the observational pilot, some classifications were sensitive to changes in temporal window or processing, while other quantities could remain stable conditional on the event retaining QPP support.

The pilot is intentionally too small to establish how general these behaviours are. Its role is instead to define the next question: whether the same patterns persist, disappear, or become heterogeneous when the analysis is extended to a substantially broader observational sample.

Current limits

What this does not establish

The project has not yet established observational sensitivity or specificity, an observational false-positive rate, physical ground truth for the analysed events, or a validated correction of the detection procedure.

Comparison events are not treated as known physical negatives, and numerical optimizer stability is not interpreted as proof of a unique optimum.

These distinctions are deliberate. The next stages are designed to test which claims can be strengthened and which must remain limited.

Current stage

Preparing the next observational test

The F0–F2 foundation is complete.

Current work is focused on reviewing the recent methodological and observational literature and preparing the prospective design for the next catalogue-scale stage.

The next observational analysis will test whether the robustness patterns seen in the pilot generalize beyond the initial cohort. A subsequent validation stage will use explicit ground truth and held-out evaluation to address questions that observational robustness alone cannot answer.

Open development

Follow the research as it develops

The development repository is public so that the structure and evolution of the project can be inspected as the work progresses.

It includes the Git-compatible documentary foundation of F0–F2, project governance and decision records, prospective design material, and workspaces supporting the next phases.

The repository is an active research workspace rather than a final scientific release. Intermediate material may evolve until a phase is explicitly frozen or released.

Research principles

How the project is being developed

01

Reproducibility

Methods, decisions, and scientific boundaries are recorded before results are promoted as conclusions.

02

Separation of evidence

Synthetic ground truth, observational robustness, numerical behaviour, and physical interpretation are treated as distinct evidence layers.

03

Staged validation

Claims are expanded only when the corresponding validation stage supports them.
